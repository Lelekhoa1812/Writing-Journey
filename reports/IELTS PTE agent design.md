# Build exam coaches on client-held durable sessions

A durable IELTS and PTE writing coach should **encode each exam’s official gates and criterion names in the server**, run them through a **fixed seven-step JSON specialist pipeline** before any conversational turn, and treat every chat request as **stateless**: the client resends a **compact session** while a **byte-stable system prefix** holds the rubric for that task family. **IELTS** feedback maps to **Task Achievement or Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy** on **0–9** bands with **equal quarter weights per task**, then **(Task 1 + 2×Task 2) ÷ 3** for a practice Writing estimate ([IELTS scoring in detail](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail)). **PTE** feedback must score **Summarize Written Text** as **Content, Form, Grammar, Vocabulary** (max **9** raw points) and **Write Essay** as **seven traits** (max **26**), applying **Content 0 or Form 0 wipes the item** before any other trait credit ([PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide (2026)](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf)). On **Vercel**, process memory dies between invocations ([Vercel Functions](https://vercel.com/docs/functions)), so **session truth lives in the client payload**, not in the Express worker. **Retrieval over public band descriptors is unnecessary** when the app already selects the rubric in code ([OpenAI Prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). **Tool calls belong in follow-up chat** only when the model must fetch or save data absent from that payload ([OpenAI Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling)). **Prompting** should force **verbatim evidence quotes**, **form gates first**, and **strict JSON for shape** while keeping judgment in natural-language rubric text ([OpenAI Structured Outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)).

## Official exam logic must run as gates before band language gets praised

IELTS and PTE look similar in a product UI but **score different constructs on different scales**, and a coach that averages “grammar” and “coherence” into a single **10–90** figure **misstates PTE** because Pearson reports only **overall and four communicative skills**, does not publish trait totals on the report, and gives **Content, Development/Structure/Coherence, and General Linguistic Range** up to **6 points each** while **Form, Grammar, Vocabulary Range, and Spelling** cap at **2** ([PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide (2026)](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf)). For **IELTS Academic Writing**, the **May 2023** public descriptors still govern: a script must **fully fit the positive features** of a target band, and **bolded negative features limit** the rating ([IELTS Writing band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf)). Task 1 is marked on **Task Achievement**; Task 2 on **Task Response**; both share **CC, LR, GRA**. **Task 1 ≥150 words** in about **20 minutes** and **Task 2 ≥250** in about **40** within one **60-minute** section, with **Task 2 weighted double** toward the Writing band ([Academic Writing format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing)). Hard IELTS failures—**plagiarism**, **bullet-note answers**, **off-topic content**, **proved total memorisation (Band 0)**, **copied rubric discounted**, **≤20 words (Band 1)**—should surface in a `gates` object before the model praises lexical range ([IELTS key assessment criteria](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-key-assessment-criteria.pdf)).

PTE adds **binary form rules** that a band-style coach easily misses. **Summarize Written Text** (from **7 August 2025**, **two** items, **10 minutes** each, passage up to **300 words**) requires **one sentence**, **5–75 words**, initial capital and one full stop; **not one sentence**, out-of-range length, or **all capitals** yields **Form 0** and **no score points** for that response ([Speaking and Writing format](https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/speaking-writing/)). **Write Essay** awards **Form 2** only for **200–300 words**; **120–199** or **301–380** caps Form at **1** without wiping the essay; **under 120 or over 380**, all capitals, no punctuation, or bullet-only answers zero **all seven traits** ([PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide (2026)](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf)). **Off-topic** or **significant pre-prepared scripted material** can zero Content ([Writing test tips, V4 July 2025](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/pte-academic-writing-test-tips.pdf)). After August 2025, **Content** on extended writing is **hybrid human–AI**, and essay **Development/Structure/Coherence** and **General Linguistic Range** are hybrid too—so a coach that blends those traits into one “vocabulary” score diverges from the live construct map ([Enhanced PTE Academic, July 2025](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/research-report-pdfs/2025-07--enhanced-pte-a-report.pdf)). Products should label all numbers as **practice estimates against public rubrics**, never **official IELTS/PTE results** ([IELTS sample responses guidance](https://ielts.org/cdn/computer-delivered-sample-tests-academic-writing/ielts-academic-writing-example-responses-to-parts-1-and-2-with-band-scores-and-examiner-comments.pdf)).

| Coach layer | IELTS (per task) | PTE Summarize Written Text | PTE Write Essay |
|-------------|------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Construct focus | **TA** (T1) or **TR** (T2), then CC, LR, GRA | **Summary** of passage: Content (0–4), Form (0–1), Grammar (0–2), Vocabulary (0–2) | **Argument**: Content (0–6), DSC (0–6), GLR (0–6), plus Form/Grammar/Vocab/Spelling (0–2 each) |
| Word / form gate | Minimum **150** / **250**; qualitative underlength risk | **One sentence, 5–75**; Form fail **wipes item** | **200–300** full Form; **<120 or >380** wipes; partial length bands **120–199** / **301–380** |
| Display scale | **0–9** per criterion; combine tasks **(T1+2×T2)/3** when both exist | Show **raw trait sums /9**; do not imply official **10–90 Writing** from one item | Show **raw /26**; gate **before** averaging traits |

Remediation order matches what examiners actually limit: **complete the task and pass form gates**, then **overview/position and paragraphing**, then **range and accuracy**—because **TR/TA** and **CC** failures often force **whole-band descriptor mismatches**, while **GRA/LR** tend to slide unless errors **impede meaning** ([IELTS band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf)).

## Serverless durability means the client owns memory and the rubric owns the prefix

Each model HTTP call is **stateless**: Vercel **spins functions to zero** and **creates a new invocation per request**, so **module-level session variables are not durable** ([Vercel Functions](https://vercel.com/docs/functions)). OpenAI’s **`previous_response_id`** and **Conversations API** persist chains at **OpenAI**, not inside Node, with **30-day response TTL** unless disabled, and **chained tokens still bill as input** ([Conversation state](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/conversation-state)). For a **generic OpenAI-compatible** endpoint, the portable contract is the **message array the client sends each turn**—which this product already almost has via **current task, draft, seven specialist JSON blobs, and chat tail**.

The **compact session** should **preserve exactly** what later turns reason about: **exam**, **task type**, **question or stimulus**, **latest draft**, **latest specialist scores and criterion comments**, and the **open coaching decision**. Those fields are the “exact preservation” set; summarising a **6** on coherence into “weak organisation” is an **in-compression information loss** risk documented in agent memory surveys ([Context Compression for LLM Agents, EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2396.pdf)). **Drop by deletion, not paraphrase**: superseded full drafts, duplicate rubric pasted into chat, raw chain-of-thought if exposed, and old turns beyond a short tail—optionally preceded by one structured note (“student will fix thesis, not examples”). **CliffCompaction-style** fidelity (truncate only, never rewrite) transfers well to tutoring even though it was measured on coding agents ([CliffCompaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26779)). **OpenAI’s opaque compaction item** must not be the **only** copy of scores; the UI and re-runs need **readable JSON** ([Compaction](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/compaction)).

**Prompt prefix design** should split **Block A** (stable: coach identity, refusal rules, output schema, **full rubric for one task family**, anchor snippets) from **Block B** (student task prompt, draft, precomputed **word_count**, target band). OpenAI caching reuses prefixes only when the **entire rendered prefix matches**; **summarisation or reordering earlier turns resets reuse** from the first changed token ([Prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). Put **shared instructions first**, **append** new user turns, and keep **tool definitions and JSON schemas stable** across chat calls in the same evaluation. On **GPT-5.6+**, explicit **cache breakpoints** after Block A let you cache **12-hour rubrics** while student text stays outside the long-lived write ([Prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). **Four cached system prompts**—**IELTS Task 1, IELTS Task 2, PTE SWT, PTE Essay**—beat one mega-prompt with an internal task switch for **cache hit rate** and **rubric focus** ([OpenAI Keep the prefix unchanged](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/keep-prefix-unchanged)).

Until you need **cross-device resume**, a **draft corpus beyond ~4.5 MB request bodies**, or **shared worker state**, **no application database is required** for memory ([Vercel functions skill](https://github.com/vercel/vercel-plugin/blob/main/skills/vercel-functions/SKILL.md)). **Provider compaction** is for **very large** transcripts (OpenAI’s example threshold is **200,000 tokens**); a writing coach can rely on **client pruning** for much longer ([Compaction](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/compaction)). **Vercel Workflows** matter only if **seven sequential specialist calls** must survive **deploys or timeouts** without client retry—not for the synchronous chat turn users wait on ([Vercel Workflows](https://vercel.com/docs/workflows)).

## Seven schema specialists finish the mark before chat may call tools

The existing **seven JSON evaluations** are not “tools the model might skip”; they are **known marking steps** whose outputs **are** the product. OpenAI distinguishes **function calling** (bridge to app data and side effects) from **structured output** (shape the model’s reply to the user) ([Structured model outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)). **Specialists belong in the second category**: each call returns **scores, evidence, or error lists** under **strict JSON Schema**, orchestrated by application code in **fixed order** with validation between steps. **`tool_choice: auto`** would let a model **omit a criterion**—unacceptable when **IELTS has four equal dimensions** and **PTE has four or seven traits** with **unequal raw maxima**.

The **chat assistant** is a separate orchestration: **one** model turn with **zero or a few tools**, only when the compact session lacks something the user asked for—for example **loading an old draft** the browser did not attach or **persisting a revision** if a draft store exists. Explaining a quoted weakness, suggesting a sentence fix, or citing descriptor language needs **no tool** when specialist JSON and rubric text are already in the prompt ([Function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling)). **Do not expose the seven specialists as chat-callable tools** unless the student submits a **new draft**; that event should trigger a **new pipeline run** from the client or API route, not an agent handoff graph. An **Agents SDK** network is optional packaging but does not change the rule on **serverless duration** and **client-held JSON** ([Running agents](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents/running-agents)).

Inside each specialist **system** message, enforce **ordering**: **gates → evidence quotes from student text only → scores → pedagogy fields → JSON** ([ECD AES illustration](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.15231)). **Temperature 0–0.2** on scoring passes; anchors at **band extremes** helped calibration more than mid-only anchors in one AES study ([Anchor is the Key (OSF)](https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/cbhgz_v1)). Documented tutor failure modes—**leniency**, **mid-scale compression**, **generic feedback**, **ignoring word limits**—mitigate through **negative evidence quotes**, **per-dimension scores**, and **hard gates** mirroring PTE’s **Content/Form zero** and IELTS **≤20 words** rules ([IRT-informed LLM AES (SCITEPRESS 2026)](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2026/149825/149825.pdf); [IELTS band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf)).

## Rubrics stay inline; retrieval waits until personal history outgrows the payload

For **one known task per request**, the rubric is **known before inference**. Selecting **`ielts_task2_v1`** or **`pte_essay_v2026`** in code and injecting the **public descriptor text into Block A** gives the model **complete band wording**, enables **prefix caching**, and avoids **chunking a short PDF** at **800 tokens with 400 overlap**—which can return **the wrong band adjacent** in semantic search ([Retrieval](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/retrieval)). Hosted **`file_search`** delegates **when** to search to the model; a missed call yields answers from **stale parametric memory**—the **F3 wrong-memory** failure mode in compression surveys ([Context Compression for LLM Agents, EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2396.pdf)). **Web search for band scores**, **pooled essays from other students**, and **per-turn retrieval “just in case”** add latency, storage cost, and **similar-but-wrong** chunks without improving a coach that already inlined the official scale.

**Past drafts** belong first in the **compact client record**: date, exam, task type, **full text of the latest draft**, specialist JSON, and a **one-line tracked error**. When the student asks to compare with last week, the client **attaches that draft** to the user turn—still no vector store. **Retrieval API** (application-run search with **attribute filters** for student, task, date) becomes justified only when **history exceeds request size** or questions are **open-ended** (“where do I drop articles?”) and the app cannot know which essay to ship ([Retrieval](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/retrieval)). Even then, pass **exact retrieved text** into the prompt; do not substitute paraphrase. **Copyright** of storing official descriptors in prompts or indexes was **not resolved** in the research notes—product legal review remains advisable.

## Four prompt variants align constructs, gates, and JSON to each writing task

**Prompt design** must diverge where exams diverge while sharing **evidence-first discipline** and **one JSON bundle** (`meta`, `gates`, `scores`, `evidence`, `corrections`, `model_answer`, `study_plan`, `chat_reply`). **IELTS Task 1** prompts penalise **missing overview**, **mechanical listing**, and **invented figures**; evidence targets **key-feature selection** under **Task Achievement** ([IELTS key assessment criteria](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-key-assessment-criteria.pdf)). **IELTS Task 2** maps body paragraphs to **quoted thesis elements** under **Task Response** and flags **memorised templates** without prompt engagement ([IELTS band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf)). **PTE SWT** checks **second-sentence detection**, **copy ratio** against `source_passage`, and sets **`form_ok=false`** before Grammar/Vocabulary when form fails ([PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide (2026)](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/pte-academic-pdfs/PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf)). **PTE Essay** scores **Content first**, separates **General Linguistic Range from Vocabulary Range**, enforces **one spelling convention**, and treats **200–300** as full Form—not the outdated prep article that treats **under 200 as zero** ([Speaking and Writing format](https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/speaking-writing/)).

**JSON Schema** should enforce **shape only**: enums for `exam` and `task_type`, **nullable scores** when gates fail, **evidence** rows with **criterion, quote, polarity**—not the full rubric embedded in `description` fields, which **bloats the prefix** and **invalidates cache** when schema drifts ([OpenAI Structured Outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)). **Business rules** (PTE length bands, IELTS task-weight math, **spelling maxLength on `chat_reply`**) belong in **application validation** because some providers **do not enforce** `minLength`/`maxLength` at decode ([Gemini structured output](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/structured-output)). Optional **two-pass** prompting—quotes and diagnoses first, scores second—mirrors multi-trait AES pipelines that reduced generic feedback ([Multi-trait specialization](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04941v1)). **`model_answer` genre** must match task: **single sentence** for SWT, **150–190-word report** for IELTS T1, **four-paragraph essay** for IELTS T2, **230–260-word essay** for PTE; **`corrections`** stay **sentence-level** with `preserve_thesis` so the coach does not rewrite the student’s argument.

The **user turn** carries **only per-request variables**: `question_prompt`, `source_passage` for SWT/T1, `student_text`, client **word_count**, optional `target_band`, `spelling_locale`. **Precomputed word count** prevents miscounts under time pressure. **Multi-turn chat** appends user messages; it never **rewrites Block A**. Every response includes **`meta.disclaimer`**: automated feedback against **public rubrics**, **not** certifying exam results.

## Conclusion

The durable coach is **two rhythms in one product**: a **deterministic, schema-bound marking run** that implements **each exam’s gates and trait map literally**, and a **stateless conversational layer** fed by a **client-held compact session** that never trades score fidelity for token savings. The deepest design tension is **PTE’s wipe rules and 0–6 trait widths** versus **IELTS’s holistic band “must fully fit” logic**—a single UI metaphor cannot serve both without mislabeling constructs or implying false **10–90** or **TRF** scores. **Retrieval and managed agent memory** are **deferrable complexity** until cross-device history or corpus size breaks the request payload; until then, **inlined rubrics plus exact specialist JSON** outperform semantic search on stability, cost, and examiner alignment. The open uncertainty is **calibration**: LLM AES literature shows **inflation and compression** even with rubrics, and **post–August 2025 PTE hybrid marking** has no public formula for blending human and AI traits—so production systems should show **ranges or confidence**, keep **trait-level transparency**, and treat coaching scores as **formative practice** while official outcomes still require **human IELTS examiners** or **Pearson’s scored delivery** ([Enhanced PTE Academic, July 2025](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/research-report-pdfs/2025-07--enhanced-pte-a-report.pdf)).
